9 Entryway Ideas for Small Apartments (Even With No Entryway)

Many apartments open straight into the living room, so coats land on the sofa and shoes form a border at the door. You do not need a hallway; you need one wall, one mat and a landing spot. These 9 ideas build an entryway out of almost nothing, all renter friendly.

Build the zone

1. A mat defines the drop zone. Sized to the door swing, a mat tells shoes where their territory ends. 2. Five hooks on the wall by the door beat one closet you do not have; spread them so coats hang side by side instead of in a pile, using adhesive hooks rated for the weight if drilling is banned. 3. A key bowl or small tray on the nearest surface ends the morning hunt; keys, wallet and phone land in the same spot every single time.

Shoes, bags and mail

4. A slim bench with storage under the seat gives you somewhere to sit while shoes disappear below. 5. Over-door racks on the nearest door hold bags, caps and umbrellas on space that was doing nothing. 6. A floating shelf or wall pocket for mail stops paper migrating to the dining table; sort it where it enters, recycle bin within reach.

Make it feel like a room

7. A mirror by the door bounces light into the darkest corner of most apartments and gives you the last look before leaving. 8. One small lamp or warm bulb near the entrance makes coming home feel deliberate rather than fluorescent. 9. A narrow runner leads the eye from the door into the home and protects the floor where traffic is heaviest.

Limit the zone to daily items only. Seasonal coats and spare bags belong in the bedroom closet; an entryway that holds everything holds nothing well.
