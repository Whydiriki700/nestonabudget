9 Renter Friendly Bathroom Ideas for Tiny Rental Bathrooms

Rental bathrooms are small, tiled and impossible to drill. Everything you need is within arm's reach, yet the counter holds six bottles. These 9 ideas use height, tension and the space behind fixtures, and every one leaves with you on moving day.

Claim the vertical space

1. Over-the-toilet shelving. The wall above the tank is the largest empty surface in most bathrooms. A freestanding rack holds towels and spare rolls without touching a wall. 2. A leaning ladder shelf does the same job with a smaller footprint and zero screws; rolled towels on the rungs look like a hotel shelf. 3. A mirror cabinet instead of a flat mirror swallows the daily bottles and clears the counter in one swap your landlord will never notice, since it hangs on the existing fixings in many rentals.

Tension and stick-on systems

4. A tension pole caddy in the shower corner holds every bottle at hand height and never slips the way suction cups do. 5. A tension rod under the sink or across the tub end hangs spray bottles by their triggers and frees the cabinet floor. 6. Adhesive hooks on smooth tile carry robes and towels; check the tile is flat and glazed, warm the adhesive with a hair dryer before loading it, and it will hold for years yet peel off clean.

Counter and cabinet order

7. One caddy under the sink for all cleaning gear: grab it, clean, put it back. 8. Roll towels instead of folding. Rolled stacks fit the narrow shelves rentals give you and do not topple. 9. A tray on the tank or counter groups the daily items into one station; a tray is the difference between clutter and a setup.

Measure before you buy: bathroom storage fails on centimeters. Note the width beside the toilet, the height above the tank and the depth under the sink, then shop with those three numbers in your phone.
